## project
What is the project, and who was it for? (real client, self-initiated, college brief, redesign exercise)

> Learnara — a self-initiated e-learning mobile app UI/UX case study, published on Behance in July 2024. Positioned as "a unified e-learning platform that bridges the gap between learners and educators", for students taking recorded and instructor-led courses. Not a client project.

## role
What exactly was your role? Did anyone else work on it, and on what?

> Solo — sole author on Behance, and the case study covers the whole arc herself: problem framing, competitive analysis, user persona, user flow, design system, low- and high-fidelity wireframes, and final UI. Built in Figma with Photoshop for presentation art direction.

## timeline
When was it, and roughly how long did it take?

> Published 8 July 2024. NEEDS HER: how long the work itself took, start to finish.

## problem
What problem were you solving, and whose problem was it? Describe the user, not the feature.

> Her stated problem, verbatim from the case study: "Existing e-learning platforms lack the cohesion needed to meet the diverse needs of students. Fragmented functionalities hinder effective communication and collaboration, creating a barrier to a seamless online learning experience. There is a pressing need for a comprehensive solution that integrates recorded courses, instructor-led training, assignment submission, and direct communication channels."
>
> So: the user is a student who has to jump between disconnected tools to watch lectures, submit work, and reach an instructor.

## evidence
How did you know it was a real problem? (interviews, a survey, reviews you read, your own observation, or an assumption — say so honestly if it was an assumption)

> She ran a structured competitive analysis of Udemy, Skillshare and Coursera, comparing each on audience, strengths, weaknesses and cost. Findings she recorded: Udemy has inconsistent course quality and limited curation; Skillshare lacks accreditation; Coursera has high costs for specialisations and limited instructor interaction "due to course scale". Her fragmentation thesis comes from that analysis.
>
> She also built one user persona.
>
> NEEDS HER — and this is the question an interviewer will actually ask: did you speak to any real students, read app-store reviews, or was the problem inferred from the competitor comparison alone? Answer honestly. "I inferred it from competitor analysis" is a perfectly respectable answer. Claiming research that didn't happen is not.

## testing
Did anyone else use or review it? What did they say, and what did you change because of it?

> NEEDS HER. The published case study has no usability-testing section. If no testing happened, write "n/a" — saying so plainly is much better than implying validation that doesn't exist. The Behance post has 20 appreciations, but that is audience feedback on the presentation, not usability evidence.

## rejected
What did you deliberately decide NOT to do, and why? (this is the question that separates designers from decorators)

> NEEDS HER. One visible decision worth explaining: the app carries four bottom-nav destinations — Home, Message, Courses, Profile. Direct messaging is an unusual thing to promote to top-level nav in an e-learning app. If that was deliberate, given her "communication is fragmented" thesis, it is a strong answer. Also: her competitive analysis lists Learnara's own weakness as "limited brand recognition, may lack extensive course library initially" — a self-aware trade-off worth talking about.

# DEFECT FOUND — fix before this case study goes to any employer

The user persona does not match the product, and a reviewer will catch it immediately.

The persona is "Cameron Williamson, 27, Masters in Business, Sales Manager,
Sydney, single, extrovert, tech-savvy". Her recorded core needs are:
- "Need to find people with similar skills that can help her tackle company goals"
- "View all her hirings in an overview"
- "The price of the service is very important"

And her frustrations are about finding "perfect people from past work relations,
family, friends and within my circle", which is "tedious".

Those are **recruiting** needs. This is a stock persona template — placeholder
name, stock photo, unedited copy from a hiring-product example — pasted into an
e-learning case study without rewriting it. The persona's goals contradict the
product's entire purpose.

This matters more than anything else in the study. The rest of the work is
genuinely solid: a real problem statement, a four-way competitive analysis with
cost and weakness columns, a user flow, a design system. That work reads as a
competent junior designer. The mismatched persona reads as someone who copies
templates without thinking — and it will cast doubt on everything beside it.

Fix: replace with one student persona. A working professional taking evening
courses, or an undergraduate juggling recorded lectures and assignment deadlines.
Give it needs that map to the four things her problem statement names — recorded
courses, instructor-led training, assignment submission, direct communication.
That is a 30-minute fix and it converts the weakest slide into a coherent one.
