# Case study answers — Foodbank

PRE-FILLED from her own published work:
https://www.behance.net/gallery/202746803/FOODBANK-DONATION-APP-DESIGN
(published 8 July 2024, Figma). Read slide by slide on 2026-08-13.

READ THIS FIRST: the published Foodbank case study is a template whose written
content was never filled in — it still contains `[Product]` placeholder tokens and
rows of lorem ipsum, publicly, today. See profile/PORTFOLIO_AUDIT.md section 1.

That changes what this sheet is for. The UI screens are real and usable. The
narrative does not exist yet, so almost everything below genuinely needs her.
The upside: because the screens are already designed, answering these questions
produces both a case study AND the replacement text for the published slides.

## project
What is the project, and who was it for? (real client, self-initiated, college brief, redesign exercise)

> Foodbank — a self-initiated social-impact donation app, UI/UX, 2024. Her own portfolio framing: "A donation flow that helps people understand impact before asking them to act." Built in Figma. Not a client project.
>
> That framing line is genuinely good — it states a design thesis, not a feature. It should be the spine of the whole case study.

## role
What exactly was your role? Did anyone else work on it, and on what?

> Solo — sole author on Behance. Covered information architecture, user flow, and the full mobile UI.

## timeline
When was it, and roughly how long did it take?

> Published 8 July 2024, same day as Learnara and Bounceless. NEEDS HER: how long Foodbank itself took.

## problem
What problem were you solving, and whose problem was it? Describe the user, not the feature.

> NEEDS HER — and this is the single most important answer on the sheet, because the published slide currently reads "The design of [Product] looks increasingly outdated when compared to its competitors", which is template text about a redesign brief and says nothing about donors.
>
> Her own one-liner already implies the real problem: people are asked to donate before they understand what their money does. Write that out properly — who the donor is, what they see today, and why they hesitate.

## evidence
How did you know it was a real problem? (interviews, a survey, reviews you read, your own observation, or an assumption — say so honestly if it was an assumption)

> NEEDS HER. The published User Research slide shows a "10+ Users" badge and a question card reading "Why would you use a donation app?" — but every answer under it is lorem ipsum.
>
> So: did you actually ask 10+ people that question? If yes, what did they say — that is real research and it should be written down. If the "10+ Users" was part of the template, say so and write "n/a". Do not leave a claim of 10+ users standing with no answers behind it; that is the version an interviewer will catch.

## constraints
What constrained you? Time, budget, tech, an existing brand, a client's opinion, your own skill gaps at the time?

> NEEDS HER. Nothing in the published study. Likely: no developer, no real donation data, self-set scope, three case studies finished in the same period.

## first_attempt
What was your first attempt, and what was wrong with it?

> NEEDS HER. Not documented.

## changes
What changed between the first version and the final one, and what made you change it?

> NEEDS HER. Not documented. One concrete thing worth recalling: the final home screen leads with a campaign card showing a photo, "Fresh Food for Improving lives in Africa", a progress bar at 80%, "$540,000" raised and "8 Hours left" — impact and urgency placed above the donate action. Was that deliberate, and did it come first or later? If deliberate, it is exactly the "understand impact before acting" thesis made visible, and it is the strongest thing in the project.

## testing
Did anyone else use or review it? What did they say, and what did you change because of it?

> NEEDS HER. No usability-testing section exists. "n/a" is a fine answer.

## rejected
What did you deliberately decide NOT to do, and why? (this is the question that separates designers from decorators)

> NEEDS HER. Visible decisions worth explaining: the app has a Wallet and a Transaction History as top-level destinations, which is unusual for a donation app — most just take a card payment per donation. If that was a deliberate choice about donors wanting to see their giving history, it is a good answer. Also: sign-up offers email/password and Google, and asks for Location. Why was location needed for donating?

## outcome
What was the outcome? Any number at all — users, screens shipped, time saved, client signed off, marks awarded. If there is no number, say who ended up using it.

> Published on Behance 8 July 2024. NEEDS HER: total screens designed. The IA shows five top-level sections (Donation List, Wallet, Home, Transaction History, Settings) plus a full onboarding and auth flow, so the real count is likely substantial and it is currently unstated.

## reflection
What would you do differently now, with what you know today?

> NEEDS HER. The honest and strongest answer available: "I used a case-study template and shipped it before replacing the placeholder copy. I'd write the narrative first now, and only lay it into a template once the thinking is done."
>
> That answer is uncomfortable but it is far better than the alternative. A junior designer who caught their own mistake and can explain it reads as someone who improved. Placeholder text discovered by the interviewer instead reads as carelessness.
